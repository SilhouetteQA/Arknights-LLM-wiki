## 台词

Learn a tera fact over a chug bottle of soda.
Number five, who was the first car crash victim?
In 759 Victoria, an engineer yanked a lever,
and the first wheeled, original, external combustion engine
yeeted itself into a wheat field.
Though it only lasted four seconds before breaking apart,
its flying wheels still found a victim, little George.
The lone scarecrow in the field, who literally took one for the team,
but poor little George could never have imagined how,
in the next 300 years, people would mount entire cities
onto originally empowered vehicles.
Nomadic cities won the race against catastrophic clouds,
and this victory redrew the entire face of terror.
Explorers could now reach farther corners of the world.
Envoy's gained the means to weave between nations.
Even city maps carried by messengers were changed.
They must now include plenty of timetables.
But many argue that the true rightful holder of the title,
the first car crash victim,
should be the director of the Gaulish Empire's first arsenal.
He lost his left leg while inventing the internal combustion engine,
and therefore decided to place every car's control pedal
on the right side before they roll off assembly lines.
Another dubious first was Captain Fleming,
who dedicated his life to making vehicles fly.
When he got into an accident in Columbia,
he reached a whopping speed of 430 kilometers per hour,
a record that still stands today.
According to rough estimates,
this land sees around 3,000 car accidents every day.
Originium leakage from these accidents
has become a major cause of aripethy infections.
And every year, this results in a number of infected
comparable to the total casualties of a regional war.
Perhaps ever since the fall of little George,
the Originium industry has been running at full throttle.
But look at these horrific stats.
Maybe it's time we remember the other pedal we've got called the break.
Maybe it's time to slow things down a bit.
